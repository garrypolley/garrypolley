The loan leaves the market. You repay it from your paycheck; principal and interest go back into the 401(k) and then earn the expected return. Interest is extra after-tax cash, not free market return.

Typical IRS §72(p) cap is the lesser of 50% of the vested balance or $50,000 minus the highest outstanding in the prior 12 months. Repeating starts another loan when the term ends — a just-paid-off $50,000 loan usually cannot be fully reborrowed the same day.

“Ahead” means extra after-tax paycheck dollars (loan interest) were deposited into the 401(k). That is not free market return — take-home pay funded it.
